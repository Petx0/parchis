# Strength v2 — review of the self-play program and the plan to break the plateau

**Date**: 2026-10-31. **Scope**: the AlphaZero-style stack under `parchis/az/` (encoding, net,
expectimax search, self-play round loop, evaluation), its 132-round 2-player history
(`docs/AZ_DESIGN.md`), and the path to a strong 2-player and then 4-player agent on the M4 Mac.
Every number below was measured today against the round-23 champion
(`runs/selfplay_2p_v1_champion/`) with the scripts in `scripts/diagnostics/` — re-run them before
trusting a number that may have drifted.

## Outcome

*Update, 2026-11-01: V2.0c — 20 more rounds of the V2.0b recipe from its champion — stayed level with V2.0b at all four ladders (49.5–50.1% head to head, 8,000 games): the V2.0b gain was a step, not a slope, and the recipe is saturated at ~72% vs the heuristic. C6 throughput is in the main tree (80 games/s on 8 workers, a 6,000-game round in under 2 min) and C2 deep targets are coded; V2.1c (C2, gated against the V2.0c endpoint) is the next run. §10.5–10.6.*

*Update, 2026-11-02: V2.1c (C2 deep targets) beat the V2.0c control 52.5% [51.8, 53.2] over 20,000 fresh-seed games (10,000 duplicate pairs), after its own four ladders had read 47.0 / 51.6 / 50.5 / 50.0% over 2,000 games each. It is the new best-so-far, by a small margin; §10.7.*

V2.1c stays the best net. C8 luck-adjusted scoring was built (unbiased, multiplier only ×1.3); §10.8–10.9.*

The 109-round plateau was not a ceiling of the architecture or the data scale. The loop could not
move, for two independent reasons that are both fixable in a day, and it was spending most of its
compute on the wrong things:

1. **The self-play generator played at random strength.** The acting temperature (1.0 → 0.25) and
   Dirichlet noise (ε = 0.25) were copied from AlphaZero, where they apply to visit counts in the
   hundreds. Here they are applied to win probabilities whose per-move differences are ~0.02–0.04.
   At that scale softmax(values/0.25) is uniform: the move actually played matched the search's own
   best move **36.2%** of the time (uniform random: 33.7%), and the exploring champion wins **5–6%**
   of games against its own greedy version (uniform random wins 2.7%). Every "self-play" position
   for 132 rounds came from near-random play, and every outcome label was the result of a
   near-random game. The policy target had the same bug: its normalised entropy is **0.996**
   (1.0 = uniform), so the policy head was trained to predict noise.
2. **The promotion gate froze the lineage.** With 600 duplicate pairs the gate needs ≥52.8% to
   promote; a candidate that is truly 1pp better promotes 10% of the time, one that is truly equal
   promotes 2.5% of the time. Three promotions in 132 rounds is the false-positive rate. Because the
   champion never changed, every round from 24 to 132 was the *same experiment* (champion + 5 epochs
   on data generated by that champion) re-run with different noise — which is exactly what the
   ladders found (round 10 ≈ round 23 ≈ round 67 ≈ round 132). AlphaZero, KataGo and TD-Gammon all
   train without a gate for this reason.
3. **Compute**: one of ten cores was used (8 workers give 5× wall-clock on a short run, ~8×
   steady-state), 58% of generation time is the encoder (36% in one feature), and the net forward is
   11%. Depth-1 search also carries almost no information beyond the raw net (its root value is the
   net's own max-biased estimate, +1.8pp vs depth 2), so the "expert" in the expert-iteration loop
   was the apprentice.
4. **Free strength never collected**: the same champion played at depth 2 beats itself at depth 1
   **57.1%** [53.7%, 60.5%] (800 games). Every gate and ladder in the project ran at depth 1.

The plan (§5) is: fix the temperature scale and drop the gate (one day, no new infrastructure,
with a go/no-go test); then parallelise generation and cheapen the encoder, generate targets with
depth-2 search on a fraction of decisions, train on afterstates so search leaves are
in-distribution, and add luck-adjusted scoring so evaluation has the power to see 2pp effects; then
run a continuous (ungated) 2-player loop at 30–50k games/round with a ladder every 5 rounds; then
4 players. The Mac is sufficient for all of this; the cloud decision rule is in §7.

## 2. Diagnosis

### 2.1 The generator played at random strength (M4, M5, M6)

`targets.py` applies softmax(move_value / τ) with τ annealed 1.0 → 0.25, then mixes in
Dirichlet(α=1) at ε=0.25. Move values are win probabilities; the median gap between the best and
second-best move is 0.024 (M6). exp(0.024/0.25) = 1.10, so the "preferred" move gets ~52% vs 48%
in a two-move decision *before* a quarter of the mass is replaced by uniform noise. The anneal
makes the first 15 plies flatter still. Result: the recorded seats played essentially uniformly at
random for the whole game, in every round, including the Phase-2 → Phase-3 rounds that did improve.

Consequences for the training signal:
- The state distribution is that of random-vs-random (and random-vs-heuristic) games, not of
  competent play. The value net was being asked to generalise from positions a strong player never
  reaches to positions it reaches at evaluation time (greedy depth-1 vs greedy depth-1).
- The outcome half of the value target (`(1−λ)·z`) is the result of a random game — pure dice.
- The policy target is uniform (M6), so the policy head (weight 1.0 in the loss, vs 2.0 for value)
  pulled the shared trunk toward predicting nothing.

This is a units error, not a tuning question: AlphaZero's τ=1 acts on visit counts (e.g. 700 vs
100 → 87.5% vs 12.5%); its Dirichlet α is set to ~10/branching and ε=0.25 dilutes a *peaked*
prior. Here the quantity under the softmax is in [0,1] with gaps ~10⁻². A dice game also needs far
less injected exploration than Go or chess: the chance node already randomises every trajectory
(TD-Gammon used none). The sweep in M5 says τ≈0.02 with no Dirichlet gives 70% greedy agreement and
an exploring side that still wins 42% of games — mild, useful exploration.

### 2.2 The depth-1 "expert" is the apprentice (M8)

Expert iteration (Anthony et al. 2017; AlphaZero) works when the search produces a policy and value
materially better than the raw net. At depth 1 the search evaluates each legal move's immediate
afterstate with the net and takes the max: no chance node is resolved, no opponent reply is
considered. The root value is therefore `max_i V_net(child_i)` — the net's own prediction with a
maximisation bias (+1.8pp vs depth 2, +2.9pp vs independent rollouts). Blending it into the target
at λ=0.5 feeds the net back half of its own biased estimate every round. Depth 2 (root move →
6 faces → opponent's best reply → net) is the first depth at which the search adds information
the net does not already have; M9 shows that information is worth ~7pp at play time.

The escalation mechanism tried to use depth-2 data but (a) one round in four, diluted by a 3-round
replay window with two depth-1 rounds, (b) judged by the gate in §2.3, (c) with the random acting
policy of §2.1 still in place. Its 0/16 record says nothing about whether depth-2 data helps.

### 2.3 The gate froze the lineage (M10)

Champion unchanged from round 23 to round 132. Each round: start from the same weights, train 5
warm-start epochs on ~18k games generated by that same champion (plus recent candidates as
opponents since round 68), compare to the champion on 600 pairs. Every candidate is an independent
draw from one distribution — the four ladders in AZ_DESIGN.md show exactly that (checkpoints
bouncing around a fixed band with overlapping CIs). Three promotions in 132 rounds (2.3%) matches
the gate's 2.5% false-positive rate; the ladder's finding that round 10 was already as strong as
round 23 suggests the genuine gain over Phase 2 happened in the first ~10 rounds (new, broader data
distribution) and the later promotion(s) were noise.

Gating was introduced by AlphaGo Zero (55% over 400 games, a deterministic game). AlphaZero
removed it and always generates with the latest network; KataGo and TD-Gammon never had one. In a
dice game where a round's true improvement is ~1pp, a gate that needs 2.8pp is a ratchet that only
turns on a lucky draw, and while it does not turn, no new data distribution is ever produced.

### 2.4 Compute went to the wrong places (M1–M3)

- One process. 8 worker processes give 5× on a 24-game shard including startup, ~8× steady-state
  (M3). This alone turns 6,000 games/round into 50,000 at the same wall-clock, which is the number
  the original plan asked for.
- The encoder costs more than the search and the net combined: `_capture_threat_scores` enumerates
  the opponent's legal moves for all 6 faces plus the 10- and 20-square bonuses on every encode
  (~8 `get_legal_moves` calls per opponent), and `get_legal_moves` recomputes `get_blockades` from
  scratch every call. A depth-1 decision does ~4 encodes; depth 2 does ~55.
- 79% of the first 40 rounds' wall-clock went to escalation rounds whose result the gate could not
  read.

### 2.5 Search leaves are out of distribution (M7)

Every training example is a post-roll state (exactly one dice/bonus slot set in the turn-context
block). Every depth-1 leaf (and most deeper leaves) is a *pre-roll* state after `next_player()`,
with no dice slot set — an input pattern the net has never seen. It extrapolates well (corr 0.997)
but with a 1.5pp mean absolute error, the same size as the median decision margin (2.4pp). The
Phase-2 calibration gate (ECE 0.002) was measured on decision states, not on leaf states, so it
does not cover what search actually queries.

### 2.6 Evaluation has the right tools and the wrong power (M10)

Duplicate pairs, Wilson intervals, a fixed ladder and Bradley-Terry ratings are the right
instruments. But seat-swapped duplicate play buys ~1.0–1.1× variance reduction here (trajectories
diverge after the first differing move, so the shared dice sequence stops cancelling luck), 600
pairs cannot see <3pp, and the puzzle suite has 10 positions. GNU Backgammon's answer — estimate
each roll's luck with the evaluator and subtract it from the result — is directly applicable
(§4, C8) and typically gives several-fold variance reduction.

### 2.7 What is sound and stays

The engine and `snapshot()/restore()`; the colour-invariant path-relative encoding; exact
expectimax over 6 faces with max^n on a win-probability vector (correct for 2–4 players); batched
leaf evaluation; the value head as a calibrated seat-win distribution with an ECE gate; the
duplicate/ladder/Bradley-Terry infrastructure; the tactical puzzle suite; the test discipline
(437 tests; every past bug has a regression test). None of this changes in v2.

### 2.8 Why the 2026-08-29/31 interventions could not show an effect

Escalation-off, pool broadening, rollout-refined targets (dose 0.05% of decisions) and the aux head
were all evaluated inside the loop of §2.1 + §2.3: a random-strength generator and a gate that
cannot detect their plausible effect sizes. Their null results are uninformative, not negative.
The aux head is free supervision and measurably learns; keep it on in v2.

## 4. Design changes for v2

Each change: what, why, expected effect, how it is verified. Code pointers are to today's files.

**C1 — Put the temperatures on the win-probability scale** (`targets.py`, `config.py`).
Acting policy: τ annealed 0.03 → 0.015 over the first 15 plies, Dirichlet ε = 0 (the dice provide
the diversity). Policy target: τ_target = 0.015 — about the net's own leaf noise (M7), so moves
within noise of each other get similar mass and clearly better moves get most of it (implied
P(best) ≈ 0.8 at the median gap). Expected: self-play greedy agreement 65–75% (M5), exploring side
≥40% vs greedy, policy-target entropy ~0.6–0.8. Verified by logging both per round (§6) and by
`exploration_sweep.py`.

**C2 — Generate targets with chance-resolved search on a fraction of decisions** (the uncommitted
`deep_fraction` change, adjusted). Play every decision with depth-1 search (cheap); on a random
`deep_fraction` (start 0.25, lower to 0.15 if round time exceeds budget) run depth 2 and play its
move. Record: deep decisions → policy target (from depth-2 move values at τ_target) and value
target `(1−λ)·z + λ·V_d2(root)`; cheap decisions → value target only (`λ=0`, outcome only; the
depth-1 root is the max-biased quantity we are removing), policy loss masked. This is KataGo's
playout-cap randomisation. Expected: bootstrap bias (mean over a round of V_d2[mover] − z[mover])
within ±0.5pp, policy targets that carry information, and an expert that is actually stronger
than the net (M9). Verified by the per-round bias monitor (§6) and by the uncommitted tests plus a
test that cheap decisions carry zero policy weight.

**C3 — Train the value head on afterstates too** (`selfplay.py`, `train.py`). For each recorded
decision also record the afterstate of the move actually played (pre-roll, encoded from the
next-to-move seat's perspective) with the same blended value target rotated to that seat, policy
loss masked. One extra `encode` per decision, roughly doubles rows. This makes depth-1 leaves
in-distribution (M7) and is TD-Gammon's unit of evaluation. Verified by re-running M7 on the v2
net (expect mean abs error well under 1pp) and by adding leaf states to the ECE gate.

**C4 — Remove the promotion gate from the generator; track strength separately**
(`round_loop.py`). Always generate the next round with the latest net. Every 5 rounds run a ladder
against fixed anchors (tuned heuristic, Phase-2 bootstrap, round-23 champion, plus the two previous
ladder snapshots) with ≥1,000 pairs and luck adjustment (C8), at both depth 1 and depth 2; keep a
"best-so-far" checkpoint by ladder for deployment only. Regression rule: roll the generator back to
best-so-far only if the latest loses to it by a CI-confirmed margin on two consecutive ladders.
Expected: a monotone ladder trend over 20 rounds (never observed so far). Verified by the ladder.

**C5 — One training run, not 132 restarts** (`round_loop.py`, `train.py`). A single AdamW with a
global schedule (constant 3e-4 then cosine to 3e-5 over the planned run), a fixed number of
optimiser steps per round sized to ~1 epoch over the round's new rows plus an equal number of
sampled replay rows, replay window 8–10 rounds (recency-weighted sampling), weight decay as now,
`value_loss_weight` 2.0, `aux_loss_weight` 0.2 (already built, measurably learns). Validation split
by game is kept for monitoring only. Rows stored as float16 shards in a local non-iCloud directory
(the `~/parchis_training_archive` precedent).

**C6 — Throughput** (`selfplay.py`, `encoding.py`, `rules.py`, `board.py`).
(a) Generation in 8 spawn workers, one shard each; weights passed as an `.npz` path (M3: 5–8×).
(b) Cache blockades on the board, invalidated by `add_piece`/`remove_piece`/`restore` (M2: ~18%).
(c) Replace `_capture_threat_scores`' legal-move enumeration with a direct geometric scan (for
each opponent piece, each face 1–6 or 7, destination via `calculate_new_position`, blockade and
capture checks), falling back to enumeration only for the bonus-20/10 follow-ups when a capture or
finish face exists. Must be byte-identical to the current function on 100k random states — the
existing property-test pattern. Expected: encode from ~230µs to <80µs; combined with (a),
≥50 games/s at depth 1 and ~20–25 games/s with `deep_fraction=0.25`.
(d) Only if (a)–(c) are not enough: move `rules.get_legal_moves`/`compute_path` to Cython or Rust
(10–50×); this is the lever to pull before the cloud.

**C7 — Play and evaluate at depth 2; offer depth 3** (`agent_spec.py`, ladder configs). Deployment
and ladder at depth 2 (M9: +7pp today for free). Add Star1 pruning on the mover's channel (values
bounded in [0,1]) so depth 3 is affordable interactively; re-run the puzzle depth sweep once the
suite has 40+ positions.

**C8 — Luck-adjusted scoring in `duplicate.py`/`ladder.py`.** For every roll by seat *s*,
luck = V_net(state, roll)[s] − mean over the 6 faces of V_net(state, face)[s] (one batched forward
of 7 rows). Adjusted result = outcome − Σ luck per seat; report raw and adjusted win rates, and
measure the variance-reduction multiplier with the existing `measure_variance_reduction` on a
heuristic-vs-net pairing. Expected ≥3× (M7's roll spread of 0.059 per decision is the luck being
removed). Size gates and ladders from the measured multiplier; aim for power to see 2pp.

**C9 — Telemetry that would have caught all of this** — see §6.

**C10 — Capacity A/B, later.** 2×256 is probably not the binding constraint (losses were flat for
reasons above). A/B 3×512 at round ~30 of V2.2 only if the ladder trend flattens.

*Revised 2026-11-02, after §10.6–10.9.* The trigger has fired: the ladder is flat at depth 1
(V2.0c) and with deep targets (V2.1d ties V2.1c), so C10 runs now instead of inside V2.2, which was
never launched. The original A/B and the §10.3 fallback ("3×512 and 30k games/round") are changed in
four ways so that one variable moves at a time:

1. **Capacity only; data volume is held fixed.** The fallback bundled 3×512 with 30k games/round,
   so a gain could not be attributed. V2.0c showed volume alone does nothing at this net size.
   Both arms keep the V2.1c recipe: 6,000 games/round, `deep_fraction` 0.25, depth 2, replay
   window 3, afterstate rows, same training hyperparameters and seeds. A volume test (30k
   games/round) follows only if capacity wins, since a larger net may then be data-limited.
2. **A matched control arm.** 3×512 (≈ 640k parameters, ≈ 5× today's 125k) cannot warm-start
   from a 2×256 champion, and every round's training warm-starts from the previous champion's
   weights. Comparing a fresh large net against V2.1c would confound size with starting point.
   Arm A is a fresh 2×256 and arm B a fresh 3×512. Both are first trained from random weights on
   the same three rounds of V2.1c self-play (deep targets), with the same epoch cap and early
   stopping, then run 20 rounds of the recipe. Arm A doubles as the missing seed replicate of V2.1c.
3. **A pre-check before the long run.** On those three rounds, fit both sizes and compare
   validation loss; repeat with 1 and 2 rounds of data to see whether the larger net is
   data-limited. If 3×512 does not lower validation loss, or widens the train–validation gap,
   capacity is not binding: skip arm B and record that.
4. **Gate.** 10,000-pair duplicate matches at depth 1, raw score (§10.8), seeds 70000+i. Arm B
   passes if it beats both V2.1c and arm A with a Wilson lower bound above 50%. If arm A itself
   beats V2.1c, run-to-run spread is larger than assumed and only B vs A counts. Strength is
   compared at equal search depth, not equal time; the large net's per-move cost (≈ 3–5×, estimate)
   is reported for deployment.

Costs (estimates): pre-check about 30 min including regenerating the V2.1c rounds (the raw archive
was deleted); arm A about 100 min; arm B about 5–6 h. Arms run one after the other, not in
parallel, so that throughput numbers stay clean.

**C11 — 4 players** — see §5, V2.3. No search or encoding redesign is needed; the cost is
generation time (263 turns/game, 3 opponents in the threat scan, 380-wide encoding ⇒ ~3–4× a
2-player game) and evaluation protocol.

## 5. Phased plan with gates

**V2.0 — Does the loop move once the generator plays sensibly? (≤1 day, config + ~30 lines)**
Start from the round-23 champion. `tau_start=0.03, tau_end=0.015, dirichlet_epsilon=0.0,
tau_target=0.015, enable_escalation=False, aux_loss_weight=0.2, deep_fraction=0.0`; bypass the
promotion gate (always adopt the candidate); 6,000 games/round as today; 20 rounds (~3.5h at
today's throughput). Ladder at rounds 10 and 20: latest vs round-23 champion and vs tuned heuristic,
1,000 pairs, depth 1. **Gate**: by round 20 the latest beats round 23 with a Wilson lower bound
above 50%, and the round-20 result is at least as good as round 10. If this fails, the diagnosis
above is incomplete — stop and investigate (first suspect: the value target still half-bootstraps

## 7. Compute budget and the cloud decision rule

Everything above fits the M4 (10 cores, 24 GB, MPS). Expected round cost after V2.1 at
`deep_fraction=0.25`, 8 workers, cheaper encoder: ~20–25 games/s ⇒ 30k games in ~20–25 min;
training on ~3–6M float16 rows/round takes 2–4 min on MPS; a 1,000-pair ladder at depth 2 takes
~2 min on 8 workers. That is ~2 rounds/hour, ~40 rounds/day of unattended running.

Move to the cloud only if, after V2.1, (a) sustained generation is below 15 games/s at the chosen
`deep_fraction`, or (b) 4-player rounds exceed ~1.5h at 20k games. The loop is embarrassingly
parallel at the game level and the net is tiny, so the right cloud shape is a many-core CPU VM (no
GPU) running the same worker code writing shards; C6(d) (Cython/Rust engine) is the cheaper lever
to pull first.

### 10.8 C8 luck-adjusted scoring: built, unbiased, and not worth its cost yet (2026-11-02)

**Result: the adjustment is exact in expectation and agrees with the raw score on every pairing
tried, but with today's nets as the evaluator it removes only 24% of the outcome variance (×1.3)
while costing 1.75× per game; the depth-1 search value as evaluator removes 56% (×2.3) at 6.8× the
cost. Plain duplicate games stay the most precise estimator per second, so the protocol is
unchanged: raw score, 10,000 pairs for effects of 2–3pp.** The multiplier itself is a new,
cheap measurement of value-head sharpness. A side finding pays immediately: OpenBLAS threading
made a 5–6-row forward 10× slower on a busy machine; capping the evaluation pools to one BLAS
thread makes plain ladder/head-to-head games 1.6× faster.

What was built (`parchis/evaluation/luck.py`, hooks in `arena.play_one_game` and
`duplicate.play_duplicate_*`, `encoding.encode_all_rolls`, `scripts/v2/ladder.py` and `h2h.py`
options `luck=default|<model.pt>` and `luck_depth=0|1`): for the roll r in pre-roll state S,
luck_r[seat] = V(S, r)[seat] − mean over the six faces of V(S, f)[seat]; adjusted result =
outcome − Σ luck_r. Because the die is fair, E[luck_r | S] = 0 for *any* evaluator, so the
adjusted score is unbiased by construction and only its variance depends on V (a control variate
with known mean; the §4 note that a biased net could bias the judge was wrong). `LuckTracker`
wraps `game.dice.roll`, so dice, moves and raw results are identical with and without it; the
six post-roll rows come from one encode (the roll touches only the turn-context one-hot). Tests
(`tests/test_luck.py`, 9): tracker leaves the game unchanged; per-roll luck sums to exactly zero
over the faces and to zero across seats; the six-streak handed to the evaluator equals the
turn's roll index; `encode_all_rolls` equals six `encode` calls on 300+ real positions
(2/3/4 players); the fast net path equals the duck-typed path with a real net.

Measurements (V2.1c champion as the evaluator unless stated; 1,000 pairs = 2,000 games per row):

| pairing | raw | adjusted | multiplier |
|---|---|---|---|
| V2.1c vs V2.0c | 51.5 [49.3, 53.7] | 51.9 [50.1, 53.6] | ×1.35 |
| V2.1c vs heuristic | 72.9 [70.9, 74.8] | 72.4 [70.7, 74.2] | ×1.3 |
| V2.1c vs round-23 | 57.3 [55.1, 59.5] | 57.8 [56.1, 59.5] | ×1.3 |
| V2.1c vs V2.0c, 400 pairs, depth-1 search value as evaluator | 49.4 | 52.1 [49.9, 54.3] | ×2.29 |

Mean |Σ luck| per game is 0.20 with the net and 0.28 with the depth-1 value, over ~170 rolls. Cost
per game, one process, one BLAS thread, same seeds interleaved: plain 76 ms, net evaluator 134 ms
(×1.75), depth-1 evaluator 522 ms (×6.8). With default BLAS threads under load the same games cost
121 / 670 / 874 ms: OpenBLAS spins up threads for the 6-row GEMM (1.7–2.9 ms per forward against
0.2 ms single-threaded) — `round_loop` already capped its generation workers; `ladder.py`/`h2h.py`
now do the same (`single_thread_blas()`).

Readings:

1. **Why so little.** With deterministic agents all randomness is dice, so under the *true* value
   function and optimal play the adjusted result would be a constant (multiplier → ∞). A multiplier
   of 1.3 says the net's value moves by roughly half of what a roll is really worth (removed share
   24% ≈ c² with c ≈ 0.5): the value head is well calibrated (bias ~0, ECE 0.005) but under-resolved
   with respect to the roll. Depth-1 search doubles the removed share (56%) because it sees what the
   roll lets the mover *do*; the raw net reads the roll only through the threat features. This is
   the same gap M8 found from the other side (depth-1 root +1.8pp over the net) and §10.4 reading 4
   asked for: a sharpness metric that calibration numbers do not give.
2. **Precision per second.** Variance multiplier ÷ cost multiplier: net 1.3/1.75 = 0.75,
   depth-1 2.3/6.8 = 0.34, both < 1 — the adjustment loses to simply playing more plain games until
   the nets are much sharper. It would win where games are expensive relative to the evaluator
   (depth-2 or 4-player matches) or where the game budget rather than time is fixed.
3. **The free gain** is the BLAS cap: ladder and head-to-head games 121 → 76 ms when the machine is
   busy; the 10,000-pair head-to-head drops to roughly 2.5 minutes on 8 workers.

**Decisions.** Gates and ladders stay on raw duplicate scores; `h2h.py`/`ladder.py` report the
adjusted score and multiplier when asked (`luck=default`), as a second opinion and as the sharpness
number. Add the multiplier (net evaluator, fixed 200-pair sample vs the heuristic) to the §6
telemetry: it should rise as the value head sharpens, and a variant whose head-to-head gain comes
with a rising multiplier is learning value, not noise. Naming: the `deep_fraction` 0.5 run launched
this morning is **V2.1d** (§10.9 when it ends; control V2.1c); what §10.4 item 4 called V2.1d (C8,
C5, telemetry) is this section plus two open items. C8 code was built in a git worktree while V2.1d
ran from the main tree and is merged after it ends.

   +1 to +2.5pp (V2.1c 52.5, V2.1d 51.2 in 10k-pair matches; continuations ~50–52 on ladders). V2.1c
   was picked as the best of the runs, so part of its margin is selection: checkpoint-to-checkpoint
   strength varies by about ±1.5pp around the recipe's level, and the last checkpoint is a sample.
3. **The ladders agreed this time.** Both continuations read below V2.1c at rounds 10 and 20, and the
   10k-pair matches confirmed the sign.
4. **Matches are fast now.** A 10,000-pair match at depth 1 takes under 3 minutes with one-thread
   BLAS, so checkpoint selection by 10k-pair matches is cheap.

**Decisions.** V2.1c remains best-so-far; replay window stays 3. The 2-player loop at this net size and
search depth is at a fixed point: deep targets (C2) were the only lever that moved it, and deep
fraction, capacity (C10) and data per step (this test) do not. Cheap ways to harvest the
checkpoint-to-checkpoint spread: choose among late checkpoints by 10k-pair matches, or average the
weights of the last few champions. Otherwise the next phase is 4 players (V2.3).
